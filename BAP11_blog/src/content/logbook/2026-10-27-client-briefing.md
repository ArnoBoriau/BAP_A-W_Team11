We received our client today: **ARCHITECT@WORK**, with the brief "Next-Level Arrival Experience". This post collects everything from the written brief and the client's pitch deck.

## Who the client is

ARCHITECT@WORK is a curated trade event for architects, interior architects, designers and other specifiers, held in 32 editions across 13 countries. Unlike a classic trade fair, it is built around innovation: every product is first approved by a committee of architects and interior architects. Uniform all-in stands (designed by C4 Creative Fo(u)r) and lounge areas instead of aisles put the focus on networking. Next to the live event, the platform runs the digital A@W Xperience community and a specialist newsletter.

## The setting

| | |
| --- | --- |
| When | 29 & 30 April 2027, live at the event |
| Where | Kortrijk Xpo, the transition zone between the entrance tent and the two halls |
| Who | About 6,000 professional visitors over two days |
| The canvas | Two walls in separate halls, facing each other about 90 m apart |

> **To check with the client:** the written brief says each wall is 21 m wide × 5 m high, the pitch deck says maximum 7 × 3 m each.

## The assignment

Today the two walls are passive room dividers. The task is to design a digital and/or interactive experience that activates them and turns them into an architectural attraction: physical, digital or hybrid, and more than decoration. The technology choice is open but must be justified through research, user insights and concept development. The concept may also use the floor around the walls, visitor movement, lighting, and a connection between the two walls across the 90 m.

The ambition is real: one or more concepts are selected and built as a live installation at the event.

**Technologies to explore:** generative visuals, AI-driven experiences, interactive projection, motion and LiDAR tracking, immersive storytelling, data visualisation, networked installations, light and sound, mixed reality, responsive environments, custom interfaces.

## What a great concept does

| Criterion | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Reinforces the A@W identity | Fits the event's black-box minimalism, professionalism, design quality and exclusivity |
| Creates an experiential journey | Turns walking in into an intentional threshold moment; visitors pause, explore, take part |
| Inspires the design community | Speaks to architects through materiality, light, form or spatial logic |
| Encourages human connection | Sparks conversation between peers, collective participation, networking |
| Demonstrates digital innovation | Current technology used robustly and with purpose; tech supports the concept, never the goal |

## Target audience

About 6,000 professionals over two days, arriving alone and in groups, looking for innovation, inspiration and meaningful encounters.

- **Practitioners:** architects and interior designers, designers and shopfitters, engineers and urban planners
- **Other specifiers:** lecturers and students, government agencies, property developers

## Do's and don'ts

**Do**

- Think beyond traditional screens
- Use technology in a meaningful way
- Embrace experimentation and innovation
- Design for a professional audience
- Consider scalability for future editions
- Work continuously during opening hours
- Ensure accessibility for a diverse audience
- Consider individuals and groups
- Allow short and deeper interactions
- Connect architecture, design, innovation and technology

**Don't**

- Make a purely promotional installation
- Need detailed instructions to interact
- Create long waiting times
- Build something fragile
- Block visitor circulation
- Be hard to operate or maintain during the event
- Rely on novelty without meaningful content

## What we deliver

| Part | What it includes |
| --- | --- |
| Concept | User and context research, concept development and validation |
| Prototype | Visual and interaction design, functional prototypes, testing |
| Presentation | Pitch deck and a final presentation or demo for client and jury |
| Extra | Technical documentation, production and implementation plan, budget estimate and supplier requirements, installation and operating guidelines, a live experience ready to deploy |

**Budget and materials:** there is no fixed budget up front. We design with production in mind, and ARCHITECT@WORK connects us with production partners, technical suppliers and industry specialists. Along the way we define technical requirements, hardware/software and operational needs, compare implementation scenarios with suppliers, and estimate budgets for different levels of execution. The goal is a balance between creativity, innovation, visitor impact and practical feasibility.
